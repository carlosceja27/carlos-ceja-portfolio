Capstone Reflection

How the work connects across the major

Read the reflection~2 min read · six courses, one thread

I came to Communication Studies after nine years of running programs, first in Army logistics and then in UX at Workday. I expected the coursework to formalize what I already knew. It did something more useful: it gave me names for patterns I had been working around without understanding them.

Seeing the frame (COMM 3). My earliest project aimed to shine a light on how the University responded to a dire situation among its students during the Fall 2021 mental-health crisis. Placing official statements beside student accounts let me practice investigative journalism, critical analysis, and interviewing, and showed me that how an institution frames a problem shapes who gets heard. It also prepares me for roles where understanding employee mental health is paramount to a business's success.

Seeing the system (COMM 119 and COMM 129). Comparing three government organizations showed how culture and channel choice decide whether information survives the trip from leadership to the front line. That was the clearest academic explanation I have found for what I experienced in government service with the U.S. Army. The graduate guide in COMM 129 applied the same thinking to people entering the workforce: boundaries, conflict, and workplace AI norms are communication problems before they are policy problems.

Measuring honestly (MGMT 113). Treating a personal habit as a prototype made me report where my own data fell short. Saying plainly what the evidence cannot support is a discipline I now hold myself to in program reviews.

Writing for the public (COMM 161). Reporting for The Santa Clara and profiling Rep. Ro Khanna moved the work from the classroom to readers who owe me nothing. Every claim needed a source, and every source needed checking.

The thread across all of it is ethical, effective communication as infrastructure. Workflows fail where messages break down, and the fix is usually clearer framing, better channels, and honest measurement, which is the work I want to keep doing as a design program manager.
